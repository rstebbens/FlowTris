FLOWTRIS FIELD GUIDE

How to play

You have five minutes to deliver as much product value as possible. You control which work starts and how your team is allocated. Production plays Tetris, automatically or with your help.

1 · Start the flow

Click any shape in Backlog to start the timer and move it into Build. New work arrives every 2 simulated seconds, keeping up to 20 pieces waiting.

2 · Manage the team

Use − / + at the top of Build, Test and Deploy to move your six people between stages. Keep the number of pieces close to the number of people for the best processing speed.

3 · Respect WIP limits

Set separate Build, Test and Deploy WIP limits using the sliders. If the next stage is full, finished work stays blocked in its current stage until space opens.

4 · Release to Production

Every 7 simulated seconds, the oldest ready piece is automatically released. Autopilot tries to fit falling shapes into the grid. Complete full horizontal rows to release their product value.

5 · Take over Tetris

Use ← → to move, ↑ or R to rotate, ↓ to accelerate and Space to drop. Using a movement key takes over from Autopilot; press A to turn it back on.

6 · Don't overflow

If Production fills up so a new piece cannot enter, it's GAME OVER. You also finish when the five-minute timer expires. Use Pause and Reset whenever you need.

Product value and effort

Blue · 10Purple · 20Yellow · 30Pink · 40Green · 50Orange · 60

Higher-value work takes longer at every stage (10-value pieces: 1×; 60-value pieces: 3×). Build takes longer than Test or Deploy. Processing times vary randomly by up to 35%. Your final score is product value released multiplied by flow efficiency: active processing time divided by active processing plus blocked and Ready waiting time. Slower shared processing counts as active time, not waiting. All unfinished items in a stage progress concurrently, sharing staff capacity (one person per item maximum). Each item's ETA is its remaining effort divided by its current processing speed, so an item with 4 seconds of effort and 25% speed shows ETA 16 seconds. ETA changes as other work enters or leaves the stage. Multiple people incur a small coordination overhead when multiple items are active; there is no separate context-switching penalty. The percentage in each column header shows effective resource utilisation, including idle staff and coordination overhead; individual item ETAs reflect processing speed and is green when balanced, amber when reduced, and red when severely reduced. This is a simplified learning simulation, not a real-world productivity forecast.
